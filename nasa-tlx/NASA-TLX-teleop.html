Part 1 — Ratings (complete after EACH condition)

Instructions. Rate the block of trials you just finished as a whole.
  1. On each of the 6 scales below, mark the point that matches your experience (click, or mark with an X on paper).
  2. Consider each scale independently — e.g. a task can be mentally easy but physically tiring.
  3. On the Performance scale only, the left end means good performance.
  4. There are no right or wrong answers. Do not discuss your ratings with other operators until all forms are collected.

Part 2 — Sources of workload (complete ONCE per operator)

Instructions. After you have tried all the interfaces, go through the 15 pairs below. For each pair, circle the scale that contributed more to your workload in these teleoperation tasks.
  • Example: if Mental Demand mattered more than Physical Demand for you, circle Mental Demand.
  • Do this once — the result weights your Part 1 ratings for every condition.

Part 3 — Scoring (experimenter only)

  1. Rating (0–100): read each Part 1 mark in steps of 5 (each tick = 5 points).
  2. Weight (0–5): count how many times each scale was circled in Part 2. The six weights must sum to 15.
  3. Weighted TLX = Σ(weight × rating) ÷ 15  (range 0–100).
  4. Raw TLX (accepted alternative, Hart 2006) = mean of the 6 ratings. Use it if Part 2 was skipped.
  5. For the paper, report per-scale means and the overall score, and state whether it is weighted or raw.

Notes for this study

  • Scale wording is the standard NASA-TLX wording (Hart & Stavig, 1988) so scores stay comparable with published teleoperation studies. The teleop examples under each scale are clarifications only — they do not change the item.
  • Fill Part 1 immediately after each condition block, before starting the next one. Order of conditions is set by the experimenter (counterbalanced).
  • Reference: Hart, S. G., & Stavig, L. E. (1988). Development of NASA-TLX (Task Load Index). In Human Mental Workload. — Raw TLX: Hart, S. G. (2006). NASA-TLX: 20 years later. Proc. HFES.
